# Chapter 2: Why Your Customers Don’t Want to Talk to You

## Core Principles
- Customers overwhelmingly prefer self-service over live phone service for a variety of issues. 
- The challenge is not getting customers to try self-service, but getting them to stay there. Channel switching (starting on the web and being forced to call) is a massive driver of high effort and disloyalty.
- Self-service sites fail because they offer too many choices and complex information, rather than a guided, simplified experience.

## Enforceable Rules
- Simplify self-service web experiences. Avoid the "paradox of choice" by guiding customers to the lowest-effort channel rather than giving them a menu of all possible options.
- Use task-based guidance rather than company-centric departmental guidance to direct customers.
- Audit self-service content for jargon and complexity. Aim for a Gunning Fog Index score of 8 or 9 (readable and clear).

## Review Questions
- Does the self-service portal guide the customer to the best resolution path based on their specific issue?
- Are we inadvertently causing channel switching by using company jargon or presenting too many support options?
- Is the content chunked and written in active voice for easy scanning?

## Examples
### Violation
- A company adds 25 different support options (chat, email, FAQ, community forum, phone) on one page, confusing the customer and leading them to simply call the contact center out of frustration.
### Good Implementation
- A company uses an "issue-to-channel mapping" tool (like MasterCard) to ask the customer what their issue is, and then directs them exclusively to the single lowest-effort channel to resolve that specific problem.

## Implications
### For Agents
- AI Agents building self-service flows must focus on reducing choice fatigue and streamlining the path to the correct answer.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- UX designs for support pages must be validated against clarity and the reduction of channel switching. Content should be tested for readability.
