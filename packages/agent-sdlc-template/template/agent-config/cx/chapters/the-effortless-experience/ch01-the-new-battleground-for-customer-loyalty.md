# Chapter 1: The New Battleground for Customer Loyalty

## Core Principles
- Delighting customers in the service channel doesn't pay. Customers whose expectations are exceeded are only marginally more loyal than those whose expectations were simply met.
- Customer service drives disloyalty, not loyalty. The average service interaction is four times more likely to make a customer disloyal than to make them loyal.
- The key to mitigating disloyalty is reducing customer effort.

## Enforceable Rules
- Do not invest heavily in "delight" or "exceeding expectations" initiatives for customer service, as they increase operating costs without returning commensurate loyalty gains.
- Target sources of high customer effort: having to repeat information, channel switching, multiple contacts to resolve an issue, and being transferred.
- Track metrics that capture customer effort and disloyalty drivers, rather than relying solely on Customer Satisfaction (CSAT).

## Review Questions
- Does this service initiative actually reduce the effort required by the customer?
- Are we tracking the sources of repeat contacts and transfers?
- Are we over-indexing on CSAT at the expense of understanding loyalty?

## Examples
### Violation
- A company encourages reps to provide free gifts and extended phone conversations to "wow" customers calling with simple issues, driving up Average Handle Time (AHT) and costs, while loyalty remains stagnant.
### Good Implementation
- A company identifies that 62% of customers call back because their issue was not fully resolved. They restructure their process to anticipate the customer's next issue and resolve it on the first call, reducing effort and improving loyalty.

## Implications
### For Agents
- Agents should prioritize fast, easy, and accurate resolution over trying to surprise or delight the customer.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- Features related to customer service platforms should be evaluated on how many steps they remove from the customer's path to resolution.
