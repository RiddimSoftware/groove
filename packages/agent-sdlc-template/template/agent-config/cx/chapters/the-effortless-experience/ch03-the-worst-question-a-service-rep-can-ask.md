# Chapter 3: The Worst Question a Service Rep Can Ask

## Core Principles
- "Have I fully resolved your issue today?" is the worst question because customers don't know what they don't know.
- A significant portion of repeat calls are caused by implicit, downstream issues (adjacent issues or experience issues) that arise after the initial contact.
- Next Issue Avoidance (NIA) is superior to First Contact Resolution (FCR).

## Enforceable Rules
- Treat customer issues as "events" rather than isolated transactions. Map out the full lifecycle of a customer issue.
- Train reps to forward-resolve high-probability adjacent issues during the initial contact.
- Measure callbacks within a 7- to 14-day window rather than relying solely on FCR or customer-reported resolution surveys.

## Review Questions
- Are reps looking past the stated problem to identify what the customer might need to call back about tomorrow?
- Are we tracking repeat contacts at the individual rep level to identify coaching opportunities for Next Issue Avoidance?
- Does the system prompt the rep with high-probability adjacent issues based on the customer's current request?

## Examples
### Violation
- A rep processes a customer's address change and immediately ends the call. A week later, the customer calls back because they need new checks mailed to the new address.
### Good Implementation
- A rep processes an address change and proactively asks if the customer would also like to order new checks and update their electronic funds transfer settings, saving the customer a future call.

## Implications
### For Agents
- Systems must be designed to predict and surface related issues (adjacent issues) to assist the rep in forward-resolving.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- Analytics implementations should track 7-day repeat contact rates linked to the original interaction, not just binary FCR status.
