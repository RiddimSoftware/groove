# Section 1: Introduction to Service Quality

## Core Principles
- Service quality is more difficult for consumers to evaluate than goods quality due to intangibility, heterogeneity, and inseparability.
- Service quality perceptions result from a comparison of consumer expectations with actual service performance.
- Quality evaluations involve not just the outcome of a service, but the process of service delivery itself.

## Enforceable Rules
- Avoid evaluating service solely based on outcomes. Process metrics must be included.
- Do not treat services like tangible goods when measuring quality.

## Review Questions
- Are we measuring the process of our service delivery, or just the final outcome?
- How are we accounting for the intangible elements of our service?
- Are we differentiating between what customers expect and what they perceive?

## Examples
### Violation
- A company focuses entirely on whether a technical issue was resolved (outcome) but ignores that the customer had to wait on hold for an hour and speak to three rude agents (process).
### Good Implementation
- A support team measures both the time-to-resolution and the customer's perception of the agent's empathy and responsiveness during the interaction.

## Implications
### For Teams
- Teams must understand that how they deliver a service is just as important as what they deliver.
### For Measurement/Analytics
- Analytics should capture metrics across the entire service journey, not just completion rates.
