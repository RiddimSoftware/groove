# Section 5: Future Research and Conclusion

## Core Principles
- SERVQUAL provides a foundational, generic skeleton that can be customized for specific organizational needs.
- Future applications should explore the relationship between SERVQUAL scores and overall customer satisfaction, loyalty, and business profitability.
- Service quality is a dynamic construct; continuous measurement and refinement are necessary.

## Enforceable Rules
- Do not treat the SERVQUAL scale as a rigid, unchangeable artifact; adapt it while respecting its multi-dimensional core.
- Continuously link service quality metrics to broader business outcomes like retention and revenue.

## Review Questions
- How does our service quality score correlate with our customer churn rate?
- What specific, localized items need to be added to our service quality measurement to capture our unique value proposition?
- Are we treating service quality as a one-off project or an ongoing organizational discipline?

## Examples
### Violation
- A tech company implements the exact 1988 SERVQUAL survey verbatim without adding any items related to digital interface or software uptime, missing critical components of their specific service delivery.
### Good Implementation
- A modern SaaS company adapts the five dimensions to their digital context (e.g., defining "Tangibles" as UI/UX design) and correlates the gap scores with their Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) retention.

## Implications
### For Product Teams
- Product development must continuously evolve the service offering in response to shifting baseline expectations.
### For Business Strategy
- Service quality must be viewed as a strategic driver of profitability, not just an operational cost center.
