# Section 2: Conceptualizing Service Quality

## Core Principles
- The "Gaps Model" defines service quality as the gap between customer expectations (what they feel a service provider *should* offer) and their perceptions of the actual service performance.
- Expectations are formed by word-of-mouth, personal needs, past experience, and external communications.
- Perceived quality is a continuum ranging from unacceptable quality to ideal quality.

## Enforceable Rules
- Always baseline customer expectations before measuring satisfaction or quality.
- Do not assume internal company standards automatically align with customer expectations.

## Review Questions
- What external factors (e.g., marketing, past experiences) are setting our customers' expectations?
- Is there a gap between what management thinks customers want and what customers actually expect?
- Are we delivering at, below, or above expectations?

## Examples
### Violation
- A product team sets a goal for page load time based on their technical constraints, assuming customers will be satisfied, without researching what load time customers actually expect from a modern app.
### Good Implementation
- A service organization conducts user research to understand customer expectations and calibrates their service level agreements (SLAs) to match or exceed those baseline expectations.

## Implications
### For Strategy
- Strategy must involve active management of customer expectations, not just improving internal operations.
### For Marketing
- External communications must be aligned with actual service delivery capabilities so as not to inflate expectations artificially.
