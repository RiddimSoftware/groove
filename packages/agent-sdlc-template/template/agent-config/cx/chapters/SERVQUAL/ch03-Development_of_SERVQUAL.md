# Section 3: Development of the SERVQUAL Scale

## Core Principles
- SERVQUAL is a 22-item instrument designed to measure service quality across five dimensions: Tangibles, Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, and Empathy.
- The original ten conceptual determinants were purified and collapsed into these five dimensions through factor analysis.
- The scale is divided into two sections: one measuring expectations and one measuring perceptions.

## Enforceable Rules
- Use multiple items to measure complex dimensions of service quality rather than a single overarching question.
- Ensure that measurement instruments capture both expectations and perceptions to calculate the discrepancy score (Perception - Expectation).

## Review Questions
- Are our customer surveys capturing the five key dimensions of service quality?
- Do our questions measure both what the customer expected and what they perceived?
- Are we relying too heavily on a single metric (like NPS) instead of a multi-dimensional assessment?

## Examples
### Violation
- A company uses a single question ("How satisfied were you with our service?") and fails to understand whether the dissatisfaction stems from a lack of empathy or a failure in reliability.
### Good Implementation
- A customer experience team deploys a customized SERVQUAL survey to identify that while they score highly on Tangibles and Assurance, their Responsiveness is severely lagging behind customer expectations.

## Implications
### For Research
- Researchers must validate their measurement tools to ensure reliability and dimensionality.
### For Operations
- Operations teams should use dimensional scores to pinpoint exact areas for training and process improvement.
