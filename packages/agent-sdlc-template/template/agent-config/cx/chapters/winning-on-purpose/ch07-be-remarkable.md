# Chapter 7: Be Remarkable, Not Merely Satisfactory

## Core Principles
- Simply "satisfying" customers is setting the bar too low. Satisfying means meeting expectations, which leads to acceptable but forgettable experiences.
- The primary purpose of a great business is to enrich customers' lives by delivering experiences so remarkable that they feel loved, causing them to return for more and bring their friends.
- Yesterday's "wow" is today's minimum acceptable standard. True leadership requires constant, customer-obsessed innovation.

## Enforceable Rules
- Do not use dynamic pricing to extract maximum value from customers; use pricing strategies to deliver the best value.
- Eliminate complex, friction-filled processes. Use digital innovation to simplify the customer journey (e.g., seamless returns, interactive 3D instructions, frictionless ordering).
- Ask the "Jenny Question": "Is there anything we could have done to make your experience more exceptional?" to Promoters and Passives to gather actionable insights for innovation.
- Avoid functional silos that stamp out deviations from the norm. Encourage frontline teams to experiment, vary approaches, and create a "wow factory."

## Review Questions
- Does this new feature or policy just satisfy the customer, or does it deliver a "wow" moment?
- Are we listening to our Promoters and Passives to find ways to make our good experiences exceptional?
- Are we relying on out-of-date innovations, or are we continually raising the bar for the customer experience?

## Examples
### Violation
- Traditional retail banking or highly regulated industries that prioritize siloed processes, risk avoidance, and compliance over customer delight, resulting in undifferentiated commodity offerings.
- A company providing indecipherable, multi-language paper instructions for assembling a complex product, leading to customer frustration and high return rates.
### Good Implementation
- Amazon launching Amazon Prime, taking a short-term financial hit to offer free two-day shipping, thereby creating a "moat" around its best customers and continually upgrading the offering (streaming, same-day delivery).
- Chick-fil-A empowering local operators to implement "Second Mile Service" (e.g., Daddy-Daughter Date Nights, Teddy Bear Overnight Experiences, unique customer recognition) to delight customers beyond food service.
- BILT providing 3D interactive, voice-guided assembly instructions on smartphones, paired with an NPS survey at the end of the process, transforming a historically frustrating experience into a remarkable one.

## Implications
### For Agents
- When designing solutions, agents should not settle for the minimum viable path if a slightly higher effort can produce an exceptional, frictionless user experience.
- Agents should seek out and recommend simplifications in instructions, processes, or configurations.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- PRs should not just be evaluated on functional correctness but also on how they elevate the user experience.
- The organization should dedicate time in sprints to "wow" innovations (e.g., performance improvements, unexpected delightful UI details) rather than just clearing the backlog.
