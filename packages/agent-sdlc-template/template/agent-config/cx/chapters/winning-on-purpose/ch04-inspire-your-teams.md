# Chapter 4: Inspire Your Teams

## Core Principles
- A company cannot sustainably love its customers without creating inspired, committed, and honored teams.
- The hierarchy of responsibilities for a great organization: obey the law, take care of customers, take care of employees, and finally, respect shareholders.
- The primary duty of senior leaders is "servant leadership"—helping frontline teams deliver great results for their clients/customers.
- Small, empowered teams provide the best environment for inspiring employees and delivering exceptional customer love.

## Enforceable Rules
- Implement regular, brief team "huddles" to gauge progress, identify blockers, and share successes based on real-time feedback, rather than focusing solely on sales targets.
- Ensure upward feedback is a trusted, anonymous process. Leaders must clear upward-feedback hurdles to be eligible for promotion.
- Never directly link customer NPS scores to employee compensation or bonuses, as this encourages gaming the system instead of genuine learning and improvement.

## Review Questions
- Do our team members feel like valued members of a team that wins with its customers?
- Are our headquarters and management structures acting as servants to the frontline workers, or are they functioning as a bureaucracy to be served?
- Are we evaluating and promoting leaders based on their ability to inspire their teams and live our core values?

## Examples
### Violation
- Companies that treat customer service centers merely as cost centers, outsourcing them to the lowest bidder, imposing strict "average handle time" limits, and ignoring the well-being of the frontline workers.
### Good Implementation
- Bain & Company implementing regular huddles and utilizing upward feedback ("How much would you like to work with this leader again?") as a strict prerequisite for promotion.
- Discover structuring its call centers into small, specialized pods, paying employees well, offering upfront tuition coverage, and ensuring executives regularly listen to and learn from frontline calls.

## Implications
### For Agents
- AI Agents should help facilitate transparent communication and feedback loops within development teams (e.g., summarizing team blockers, highlighting PR review bottlenecks).
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- Workflows should be designed to remove toil from developers. Tools should act as servant-leaders to the engineering team. Metrics like deployment frequency should be used to find and fix blockers for the team, not to punish individual developers.
