# Chapter 9: Be Humble, Net Promoter 3.0 and Beyond

## Core Principles
- Humility is the antidote to arrogance, greed, complacency, and entitlement—the "four horsemen" that derail successful companies from the inside.
- Being humble means recognizing that you always have more to learn from your customers and that true servant leadership is the prerequisite for earning loyalty.
- The primary mission of every organization should be to build a community whose purpose is to sustainably enrich the lives of customers, treating all members according to the Golden Rule.

## Enforceable Rules
- Implement the "Net Promoter (Customer Capitalist) Manifesto": Lead with love, inspire teams, unleash NPS-caliber feedback flows, nurture relentless learning, and quantify earned growth economics.
- Do not rely on "ethical AI" alone; aim for "loving AI." Algorithms and bots must be trained on objective functions that enrich customers' lives, not just maximize financial extraction.
- Incorporate "Customer-Based Accounting" into financial planning. Track active customers, defections, and "Earned Growth" (referrals vs. bought growth) alongside traditional revenue.
- Do not let technology replace human empathy. Digital signals and click patterns cannot tell you *why* a customer feels a certain way; direct conversations and feedback remain essential.

## Review Questions
- Are we acting with humility, seeking out and processing critical feedback from our customers and employees?
- Are our digital tools, algorithms, and automated processes designed to "love" the customer or merely extract maximum value from them?
- Are we tracking the quality of our growth (Earned Growth) rather than just top-line revenue?

## Examples
### Violation
- The downfall of companies featured in "Good to Great" that succumbed to the undisciplined pursuit of more, prioritizing short-term financial capitalist gains over long-term customer enrichment.
- AI models or dynamic pricing algorithms that maximize short-term accounting results by gouging customers during a crisis (e.g., airline ticket surges during a hurricane).
### Good Implementation
- FirstService leveraging its "Earned Growth" strategy, recognizing that 50-70% of new customers come through referral, and empowering local managers with equity stakes to play the long game.
- Leaders who maintain an "owner's mentality" and tie their bonuses and incentives to the long-term success and loyalty of their customers, rather than short-term stock market fluctuations.

## Implications
### For Agents
- Agents must approach problem-solving with humility, recognizing that initial assumptions might be wrong. They should actively seek out "feedback" (logs, test results, user hints) and adjust course.
- Automated scripts and bots should be designed with the end-user's experience in mind, preventing actions that technically succeed but practically degrade the user experience.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- Avoid the arrogance of "my code works on my machine." Prioritize robust testing and clear feedback loops in CI/CD.
- Evaluate technical debt and architectural decisions not just on short-term delivery but on their long-term impact on the team's ability to safely and quickly deliver value to the customer.
