# Chapter 3: Love Your Customers

## Core Principles
- "Love" in a business context means caring so much for a person that your own happiness and success derive from increasing their happiness and well-being.
- The Golden Rule is the bedrock of good business. When customers feel loved and their best interests are put first, they offer honest feedback, integrate your services into their lives, and fuel sustainable growth.
- Customer capitalism prioritizes long-term relationships and customer delight over short-term revenue optimization.
- Even in the digital era, algorithms and automation must be designed to enhance human empathy and warmth, not to replace the human element or trap customers.

## Enforceable Rules
- Eliminate all "bad profit" mechanisms such as hidden resort fees, unreasonable late penalties, artificial points breakage, and difficult-to-cancel subscriptions.
- Do not let artificial intelligence or pricing algorithms automatically extract maximum value at the expense of customer trust.
- Provide accessible, high-quality, human-led customer service options alongside excellent digital self-service tools.

## Review Questions
- Are our digital tools and bots designed to make the customer's life easier or simply to reduce our own servicing costs?
- What are the "nuisance fees" or pain points in our product that we tolerate because they generate revenue?
- How does interacting with our product/company make the customer feel? 

## Examples
### Violation
- Traditional credit card, airline, or rental car companies that rely on punitive late fees, hidden roaming charges, or complex cancellation processes to prop up their bottom line.
### Good Implementation
- T-Mobile's "Un-carrier" revolution, which eliminated contracts, roaming charges, and hidden taxes.
- Discover Card eliminating annual fees, foreign transaction fees, and points breakage, while ensuring 100% US-based customer service.

## Implications
### For Agents
- When automating processes or creating workflows, the Agent must ensure the user has easy "escape hatches" and is not trapped in an endless loop. Design error handling and feedback loops that are empathetic and helpful.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- Features that introduce friction for the user just to meet a business goal should be challenged. PRs should focus on simplifying the user journey, offering transparent options, and never hiding the path to human support or resolution.
