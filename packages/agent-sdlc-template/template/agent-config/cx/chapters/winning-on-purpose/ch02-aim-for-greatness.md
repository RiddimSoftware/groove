# Chapter 2: Aim for Greatness

## Core Principles
- NPS acts as a moral compass and a "GPS for greatness," helping organizations know when they have achieved great results for their customers.
- Greatness should be measured by human net worth (meaningful service to others) rather than merely financial net worth.
- Financial capitalism focuses on profits, which often measures value extracted. Customer capitalism focuses on creating customer Promoters, which measures value created.
- A company cannot be truly great or resilient without embracing a great purpose, and the purpose that consistently wins is enriching customer lives.

## Enforceable Rules
- Do not optimize systems solely for short-term shareholder returns, as this often leads to extracting value from customers and employees.
- Use customer feedback and NPS not as a financial stick, but to make it safe for employees to always do the right thing for the customer.
- Evaluate the success of a project or team by the loyalty and promotion it generates among its users, not just its immediate financial ROI.

## Review Questions
- Is our primary metric of success related to value extraction (profits/revenue at all costs) or value creation (customer love and loyalty)?
- Does this system or process help our frontline workers lead lives of meaning and purpose?
- Are we building a product that people will genuinely recommend to their loved ones?

## Examples
### Violation
- Companies that optimize strictly for financial metrics (as seen in some traditional "Good to Great" companies) often resort to abusive practices like punitive fees or exploiting exit barriers, leading to long-term decline and loss of customer trust.
### Good Implementation
- Companies like Charles Schwab and Rackspace use NPS to provide objective moral backstopping, ensuring their teams know that delivering extraordinary service is the true measure of their greatness, which in turn drives long-term, sustainable shareholder returns.

## Implications
### For Agents
- AI Agents should prioritize code and architectural decisions that build long-term trust and reliability for the end-user over short-term, fragile optimizations.
### For Tickets/PRs/CI
- When defining the definition of "Done" for a ticket, it should include a consideration of the user's emotional and practical outcome. PRs should be reviewed against the standard of whether they contribute to an experience the user would recommend.
