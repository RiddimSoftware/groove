# Chapter 8: Patton's Advice for Product Managers

## Premise
Patton's maxim — "Never tell people how to do things. Tell them what to do, and
they will surprise you with their ingenuity." — cuts both ways for product
managers. Customers should tell the PM what problem to solve, not how the
product should work. The PM should tell designers and engineers what the
product needs to do, not how to design or build it.

## Key Principles
- Customers cannot design your product. They don't know what's possible and
  can't envision the solution in advance; their value is in revealing the
  problem.
- When the PM focuses on the problem to solve rather than the solution, the
  set of possible answers expands dramatically.
- Designers and engineers are at their best when they own the "how." Spelling
  out implementation in a spec collapses their contribution.
- Interaction design and product management are different skills; the rare PM
  is strong at both. Don't let scarcity of designers force the PM to also
  design the interaction.
- Designers add the most value early — during problem framing and discovery —
  not after the spec is locked.

## Practices
- Treat the user experience designer as a core member of the product team:
  include them in customer visits, persona work, brainstorming, and strategy
  and roadmap decisions.
- Frame requirements in terms of what the product must do for the user;
  leave latitude on the how to the designer and engineers.
- When reviewing your own specs, audit for implementation prescriptions that
  belong to engineering or design and strip them out.

## Pitfalls
- Letting customers dictate the solution. It feels responsive but produces
  worse products.
- Treating UX design as a service function pulled in after the spec is done —
  this is where most of a designer's value is forfeited.
- Spec'ing the implementation for engineers. Engineering doesn't appreciate it
  any more than the PM appreciates customers dictating requirements.
