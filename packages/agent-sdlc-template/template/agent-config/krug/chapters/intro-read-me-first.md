# Introduction: Read Me First

*Throat clearing and disclaimers.*

## Who Krug is, and who the book is for

Krug is a usability consultant. His work is: clients send him designs / URLs / prototypes; he tries to use them, notes where users will get stuck ("expert usability review") or watches other people use them ("usability testing"); then he meets the team to describe the issues and decide what to fix. He stopped writing the "big honking report" — a live presentation lets the team push back, and Agile/Lean teams have no time to read reports anyway.

The book is written for the people who can't afford a Krug: in-house designers, developers, site producers, project managers, marketers, the people who sign the cheques, and one-person bands doing it all. It is deliberately short so that busy people will actually use it.

## What you won't find

- **Hard-and-fast rules.** The real answer to most usability questions is "It depends." The book is principles, not a checklist of dos and don'ts.
- **Predictions about future technology.** Krug's guess is no better than yours.
- **Bad-mouthing of poorly designed sites.** Examples are mostly excellent sites with minor flaws — more is learned from good examples than bad ones.

## Krug's definition of usability

Usability attributes circulating in the field:

- **Useful** — does it do something people need done?
- **Learnable** — can people figure out how to use it?
- **Memorable** — do they have to relearn each time?
- **Effective** — does it get the job done?
- **Efficient** — with a reasonable amount of time and effort?
- **Desirable** — do people want it?
- **Delightful** — is using it enjoyable, or even fun?

Krug's working definition is simpler. A thing is usable if

> a person of average (or even below average) ability and experience can figure out how to use the thing to accomplish something without it being more trouble than it's worth.

The book is short, the rules are few, and the stakes are real — building usable things means people get home in time for dinner once in a while.
