# Chapter 22 — Partnering with Sales

In B2B and many B2C contexts, sales is among the most consequential partners for the product organization. The partnership replaces the older subservient or adversarial dynamics with structured collaboration.

## The Fundamental Truth

Salespeople operate on the front lines with customers and are valuable input sources. In the product model, PMs maintain strong relationships with salespeople and actively participate in the sales process for key prospects.

But:

> "The customer and the salesperson do not know what is technically possible. They are often experts in their own domain, but rarely are they experts in the enabling technologies used to build the solutions they buy."

When customers buy your products, they are hiring you to be the experts for them. Strong products come from **combining real customer needs with solutions that are just now possible**.

## How Product Partners with Sales

### Reference Customers

The single highest-leverage thing product can do for sales: **produce referenceable customers** using the latest offering and loving it.

If sales is asking for specials, fighting product roadmap requests, or complaining about competitive losses, the root cause is often that they lack referenceable customers in the relevant segment.

### Handling Special Requests

In the product model, the greatest fear about sales is **specials** — one-off capabilities done for specific customers. It does not take many specials before a product is more complex, less nimble, and harder to learn and use for everyone.

Recommended process for special requests:
1. **All requests go through the head of sales first**, who blocks most before they reach the product team.
2. Requests that pass: PM determines if the prospect is in the target market.
3. If so: PM identifies the underlying problem and a solution serving this prospect and others.
4. If not in target market or outside the strategy: **decline** in favor of the larger opportunity.

### Competitive Losses

Salespeople hear "we lost to competitor X because we lack feature Y" often. *Every experienced product team knows customers say one thing and do another — sometimes consciously, often not.*

The right response is not feature-chasing. It is:
- Develop reference customers in the segment
- Have PM + product marketing identify what to say about the offering's strengths
- Calibrate which losses signal a real gap vs. a convenient excuse

### Customer Access

This is the most contentious area. Sales often wants to gate customer access. The book is direct:

> "Direct, unencumbered access to users and customers is absolutely essential to creating successful products. It is one of the few truly non-negotiables in the product model."

If necessary, the CEO must back this up to sales leadership.

That said, product teams must be coached on appropriate behavior. Many sales organizations offer **"charm school"** — short customer interaction training — to anyone visiting customers. Topics:
- Roles and responsibilities of sales/services people in the field
- Behavioral norms
- The rule that **any promises made will be kept**

### Coordination via Product Ops / User Research

To prevent random product teams from descending on the same customers weekly without coordination, **product ops or user research facilitates** visits — ensuring teams don't overlap and that account managers and customer success are kept in the loop.
