# Chapter 41 — Objections from Marketing

Marketing organizations often raise objections about how they'll execute when the product side moves to less predictable rhythms.

## "We can't market a moving target."

**Response:**
- The **product vision** is the stable thing to market — the future you're creating for customers. It's 3–10 years out and is the strongest brand-builder available.
- Outcome-based roadmaps replace feature roadmaps.
- Product marketing partners with the product organization on positioning, messaging, and go-to-market. The relationship is collaborative, not handoff.

## "We need to plan campaigns months in advance."

**Response:**
- Reserve **high-integrity commitments** for launch-dependent moments. The product organization can commit to a date when the date truly matters.
- For continuous improvements (most of what ships), the marketing team isn't running launch campaigns — they're amplifying customer-loved improvements as referenceable stories.

## "How do we tell the market story when features keep changing?"

**Response:**
- Market the **vision** (where we're going) and the **outcomes** (what customers achieve) — not the feature list.
- A product marketing organization that depends on feature stability for storytelling is fragile by design. Build a story around customer transformation and let features be evidence.

## "Press releases need 6-8 weeks of lead time."

**Response:**
- For major launches, plan with the product organization using high-integrity commitments.
- For ongoing improvements, build a continuous communications cadence — blog posts, customer stories, demo videos — that doesn't require lengthy lead times.

## "We don't get enough visibility into what's coming."

**Response:**
- The product organization should bring marketing into discovery — product marketing especially.
- **Customer impact assessments** for each significant release inform marketing's plans.
- Regular product reviews and stakeholder briefings give marketing the visibility they need without giving marketing edit rights on the roadmap.

## "Our pricing has to be set in advance."

**Response:**
- Pricing and packaging are joint decisions of product and product marketing. (See [Chapter 23 — Partnering with Product Marketing](ch23-partnering-with-product-marketing.md).)
- For continuous improvements that don't trigger pricing changes, the question is moot.
- For new tier or capability launches, pricing is set as part of the launch plan, with discovery input.
