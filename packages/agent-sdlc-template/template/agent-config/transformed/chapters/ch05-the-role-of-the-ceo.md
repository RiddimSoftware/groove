# Chapter 5 — The Role of the CEO

> "Probably the most important lesson we have learned in 20 years of helping companies with transformations to the product model is the absolutely critical role that the CEO needs to play in order for the transformation to succeed."

## What the CEO Does Not Need to Do

- The CEO does **not** need prior experience with the product model
- The CEO does **not** need to spend a lot of time on the transformation itself

## Why the CEO Role Is Critical

Transformation impacts the company **far beyond the technology department**: sales, marketing, finance, HR, legal, business development, compliance, manufacturing.

Not every stakeholder will be enthusiastic. Many will be motivated to try a better approach. Some will be passive resisters. A few will actively resist — *protecting their current responsibilities, or simple preference for the devil they know.*

All of these people ultimately report up to the CEO. **The CEO is the only person they will look to for the answer to "is this truly important and necessary?"**

> "The company cares about what the leader cares about." — Bill Campbell

## The Delegation Trap

Too often, the CEO delegates ownership to a CIO, Chief Digital Officer, or Chief Transformation Officer. These officers can influence within their organization, but **stakeholders rarely report to them**. The same dynamic results: transformation stalls when affected stakeholders don't see top-level commitment.

## CEOs and IT Bias

Most companies begin transformation efforts by focusing on product, design, and engineering. That's appropriate — without those capabilities established, the rest is premature.

But it's only after those changes are made that companies realize the new capabilities **impact the entire company**. When the CEO and other senior leaders are not actively supportive of those downstream changes, the transformation stalls.

## The Mandate

> "To be explicit: The CEO needs to be viewed as the chief evangelist for the product model."

If a CEO is unwilling or unable to take this role, the company can save time, money, and effort by reassessing readiness.

## The Upside

A company operating successfully under the product model improves the situation for everyone, not just product and engineering:

- Employees feel more pride in the company's products
- Marketing has more to promote and position
- Sales can sell more
- Everyone sees the financial impact
- Employee morale and retention improve

There's a strong case to be made for the active help and support of the CEO.
