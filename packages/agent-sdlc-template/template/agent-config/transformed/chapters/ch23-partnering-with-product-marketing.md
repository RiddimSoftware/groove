# Chapter 23 — Partnering with Product Marketing

Product marketing is the most natural partner for product management in any company shipping a commercial product.

The role is distinct from broader marketing — its focus is on the product itself, how it's positioned, and how it reaches its market.

## Market Understanding and Competitive Analysis

Product marketing brings to the partnership:

- Deep understanding of the market and customer segments
- Competitive intelligence — what competitors offer, how they position, where the gaps are
- Industry trends affecting buying behavior
- Voice-of-customer aggregated across sources (analyst reports, win/loss interviews, customer advisory boards)

PMs depend on product marketing for the **outside-in view**. Product marketing depends on PMs for the **inside-out view** (what's coming, why, when).

## Product Go-to-Market

Every product the team builds will need to be brought to market: pricing, packaging, positioning, launch, sales enablement, marketing campaigns, customer support readiness.

Product marketing leads go-to-market planning. The PM provides the strategic context (problem the product solves, target customer, value proposition derived from discovery).

## Key Product Decisions

Several decisions sit at the intersection of product and product marketing:

- **Target customer** — segments and personas
- **Positioning** — how the product fits in the market and against alternatives
- **Pricing and packaging** — what's in which tier, at what price, with what monetization model

These decisions need both perspectives. Done in isolation, either side gets it wrong.

## Customer Discovery Program

A common practice in B2B: a **customer discovery program** invites a small set of customers to partner with the product team on a new product or major capability — co-creating it through discovery.

Product marketing helps:
- Identify candidate customers
- Set expectations (this is discovery; the team may end up not pursuing this direction)
- Bridge to broader market launch when the work is ready

## Messaging and Positioning

Product marketing owns the messaging that customers, prospects, and the market will hear. They depend on:
- Clear positioning derived from discovery
- Differentiators that are real, not aspirational
- Outcomes the customer will achieve

## Customer Impact Assessment

A useful artifact: for each significant product release, an assessment of customer impact — who is affected, how, what training or support is needed, what's the message. Product marketing typically owns this assessment in partnership with PM.

## Pricing and Packaging

Pricing decisions are often where product marketing's expertise is decisive. PMs without pricing experience tend to default to mirroring competitors or to formulas — both usually leave money on the table or undersell the value.

## Sales Enablement

The hand-off from product to sales is via product marketing:

- Sales decks and demo scripts
- Battle cards (competitive positioning)
- Use cases and customer stories
- Training on new capabilities and on the problem they solve for which customer

When product marketing is strong, sales has a coherent story. When it's weak, sales improvises and the message fragments.
