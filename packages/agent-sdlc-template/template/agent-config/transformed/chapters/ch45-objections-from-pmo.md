# Chapter 45 — Objections from the PMO

The Program Management Office is often the visible manifestation of the prior command-and-control culture. PMO objections are among the most important because they reveal whether the transformation will be allowed to actually change how decisions are made.

## "Without project management, nothing will ship on time."

**Response:**
- Empowered teams own their delivery.
- **High-integrity commitments** cover cases where dates truly matter.
- Continuous, small, instrumented releases reduce the need for the kind of cross-team coordination project management was created to provide.
- The premise that PMO is required for on-time delivery is itself a product of the project model.

## "We need a single source of truth on what's being built."

**Response:**
- That source of truth is the **product strategy + team objectives**, not a project plan.
- Strategy documents are visible, regularly updated, and connect product work to business outcomes.
- A status dashboard at the team-objective level provides visibility without micromanagement.

## "PMO is being eliminated — what about my team?"

**Response:**
- Some PMO roles transition naturally to **product ops** or to **delivery management** roles on teams (helping with cross-team coordination, release management).
- Others may not fit the new model and need to find other roles.
- Transparency about which is which prevents both surprise and sabotage.

## "We provide governance and ensure compliance."

**Response:**
- Compliance functions remain important.
- Governance can be delivered via principles, paved paths, and team-level architecture review — not via stage-gate approvals that block releases.
- The PMO of the future looks more like a **product ops function** focused on enabling teams than a gatekeeping function controlling them.

## Warning: PMO Disguised as Product Ops

> "Another danger that we discuss in more detail later is when the title 'product ops' is used as a cover for a reconstituted program management office (PMO). This is a problem because old-style PMOs are often the manifestation of the command-and-control culture we're trying to replace."

This is one of the most common transformation failures. The PMO survives by renaming itself.

Symptoms of PMO-disguised-as-product-ops:
- Approvals and gates remain
- "Product ops" sits between teams and customers, data, or stakeholders
- Reports up to a PMO-style leader rather than to a product leader
- Holds status meetings rather than coaching teams

The legitimate product ops function (see [Chapter 13](ch13-product-leaders.md#product-ops)) is a small group of user researchers and data analysts who **coach and support** teams. It is not gatekeeping.
