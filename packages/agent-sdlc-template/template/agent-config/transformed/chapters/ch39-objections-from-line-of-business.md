# Chapter 39 — Objections from Line of Business

Line-of-business managers are stakeholders responsible for an entire business unit — a category manager, a general manager, sometimes a divisional president. Their concerns overlap with CEO concerns but with a narrower P&L scope.

Many of the dynamics in [Chapter 38 — Objections from the CEO and Board](ch38-objections-from-ceo-board.md) apply here.

## "I have P&L responsibility for this product line. Why would I not have control of the engineering resources?"

**Response:**
- A line-of-business manager is a critical partner — same way a startup CEO is — but, like a startup CEO, depends on her product and technology leaders.
- The most strategic decisions (product vision, product strategy, identifying appropriate outcomes for product teams) are made **collaboratively**.

## "Leadership expects me to know about anything that impacts my business. I need to know what's coming so I can answer questions."

**Response:**
- Not only is this true — leadership in the product model **depends on** informed, customer-focused, data-savvy, engaged business leaders.
- The line-of-business manager shares as much strategic context as possible with the product and technology leaders.
- Product teams and leaders share back the insights and learnings emerging from the front lines.
- Most important: **product managers and product leaders connect the dots** between their product work and the desired business impact.

## "I find it frustrating that product teams feel they need to repeat all the problem discovery I've already done. There's no time left for solution discovery."

**Response:**
- Acknowledge the frustration.
- **Share your problem discovery findings with the team** — that's part of the partnership.
- The product team isn't repeating work to question it. They're validating insights with the customer base and the data, extending what you brought, then doing solution discovery from there.
- The discovery work the team does is essential because it's where insights get refined into something a solution can be built against.

## Other Common Concerns (Following the Same Pattern)

The line-of-business manager will raise variations of:
- **"How do I commit to revenue if I don't control the roadmap?"** → Same logic as the CEO: product strategy + outcomes per team objective + high-integrity commitments when truly needed.
- **"My region/segment has unique needs."** → Empowered teams discover solutions that work across segments; line-of-business inputs help shape what "work across" means; specials remain rare.
- **"My P&L is the success measure."** → Empowered teams accept outcome accountability that ladders up to P&L.
