# Chapter 10 — Product Managers

> "We will warn you now that this is consistently the most difficult new competency to establish."

## Why It's Difficult

Your company very likely already has people titled "product manager." But it is also likely that these people were **simply retitled** from other roles — product owner, business analyst, program manager.

The prior model very likely had a role with the same title, but with a completely different job, skills, and responsibilities.

## What the Product Manager Owns

In the product model, the product manager is **explicitly responsible for the value and viability** of the solutions built — and is **overall accountable for achieving the product's outcomes**.

- **Value** requires deep understanding of users and customers
- **Viability** requires solid understanding of the business

## Areas of Expertise

The PM must develop expertise in:

1. **Users and customers** — qualitatively and quantitatively. How customers and users choose and use your products. Market, competitive landscape, technology and industry trends.

2. **The business** — how your product is funded, monetized, manufactured, marketed, sold, delivered, serviced — plus legal, contractual, and compliance constraints.

A normal new PM needs **2–3 months** to come up to speed on customers and business, with active coaching from a capable manager. Without that coaching, the person could be in the role for multiple years without the necessary knowledge.

## The Most Common Symptom of Incompetence

> "One clear sign that the product team lacks a competent product manager is that all decisions require either an escalation to a manager or a stakeholder meeting where the stakeholders are asked to decide."

## The Bar

Two candid realities:

> "Your head of product will be judged by her weakest product manager."

> "Your CEO should believe that each of your product managers has the potential to be a future leader of your company in the next five or so years."

While exceptions exist, **the majority of legacy business analysts and Agile-focused product owners do not reach this bar.** Those legacy jobs are narrowly defined as compared to the product manager role in the product model, with much less specific knowledge required.

## Failed Transformations

> "If you believe you can simply retitle your product owners or business analysts, you are very likely heading for failure."

Some of your existing people may need other roles. But many high-potential candidates for the PM role are scattered across your organization — identify them, get them into the role, and provide coaching.

Look for people who:
- Have broad understanding of business and prove they can quickly learn other parts
- Are comfortable immersing in data
- Want to get face-to-face with users
- Are willing to whiteboard with designers and engineers
- Love to learn fast and collaborate

## Direct Access (Non-Negotiable)

For the PM to do the job, she needs **direct, unencumbered access** to:

1. **Users and customers** — "no one" should be allowed to gate this. Not sales, marketing, customer success, user researchers, or vendor managers. Train teams on appropriate interactions, then let them go. "The magic happens" when engineers see users struggle.
2. **Product data** — multiple data sources covering how users interact with the product, how customers purchase, and how behaviors change over time. Without direct data access, the PM is flying blind.
3. **Business stakeholders** — marketing, sales, services, finance, legal, compliance, manufacturing, SMEs. The PM must establish trust with each, learn their constraints, and ensure they are considered in solutions.

## Domain Expertise

A surprising omission from the list of PM attributes: **deep prior domain expertise** is not required and may be harmful.

Shreyas Doshi's framing: *"true domain expertise is domain knowledge minus domain dogma."* Domain dogma is the litany of "all the reasons it won't work." Long-tenured industry insiders often cannot distinguish dogma from knowledge.

New PMs are expected to become expert in their domain in the first months. For specialized domains (surgical instruments, tax prep, regulatory reporting), hire a **dedicated domain expert** shared across product teams.
