# Chapter 11 — Product Designers

Most companies transforming already have designers — but they typically play a very different role than the product model requires.

In prior models, designers often play a **service role** to a product manager or **support marketing**. Neither is what the product model needs.

## Two Issues to Solve

### 1. How the Product Works

> "Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works." — Steve Jobs

In prior models, designers usually have a narrow range of skills (graphic, visual design). Helpful skills, but not enough.

In the product model, designers take on a much larger role — **responsible for the holistic customer experience and how users and customers experience the product's value**.

They are skilled in:
- **Service design** — end-to-end customer journey
- **Interaction design** — how technology and people communicate with each other
- **Visual design** — look and feel
- **Industrial design** — for products that include devices

Most product designers won't be expert in all four, but **interaction design knowledge and skills are critical** for most products.

### 2. Product Discovery

Current designers are often brought in **at the end** to "make things pretty" or apply a veneer that makes products look like they come from the same company.

In the product model, product designers help **discover the effective solution**:
- Engage directly with users and customers
- Understand how users think about the problem and proposed solutions
- Design holistic experiences that feel recognizable and intuitive
- **Intentionally design changes** so that users don't notice the constant stream of improvements — or when they do, the experience guides them through

## Designer as Discovery Partner

> "The product designer is the key partner for the product manager and tech lead in the daily product discovery work."

The designer usually leads in expressing product ideas as **prototypes** — experienceable, considerable, evaluable, testable.

A typical designer creates **a dozen or more prototypes a week**. Most get thrown away. Every one helps converge on an effective solution.

## Compensation

Due to skill required and market demand, strong product designers are compensated at the level of a product manager and an engineer.

Normally each user-facing product team has a **dedicated** product designer. As more companies move to the product model, demand for these professionals continues to grow.
