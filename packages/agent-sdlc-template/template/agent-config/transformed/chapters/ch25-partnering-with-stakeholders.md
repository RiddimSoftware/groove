# Chapter 25 — Partnering with Stakeholders

A short chapter that synthesizes principles for partnering with the various stakeholder groups: legal, compliance, manufacturing, customer success, HR, operations, business development, IT, security, and others.

## The Fundamental Shift

In the prior model, stakeholders **tell** the product team what to build. In the product model, stakeholders are **partners** with product teams in finding solutions that work for the customer and for the business.

This is not less engagement; it is **different** engagement.

## Trust as the Currency

The partnership only works on trust. Trust takes time to earn and is easy to lose. The PM is the primary face of the product team to the stakeholder — making the PM's competence and integrity critical.

A stakeholder who trusts the PM:
- Shares concerns and constraints early, before the team has built anything
- Engages in solution discussion rather than declaring requirements
- Helps the team understand the business
- Is willing to be persuaded by data

A stakeholder who doesn't trust the PM:
- Sends requirements down rather than problems
- Escalates conflicts
- Avoids the discovery process
- Hoards customer relationships

## How Product Teams Build Trust

- **Show up prepared** to every stakeholder meeting
- **Share strategy and intent** transparently
- **Surface constraints early** when discovering solutions — don't surprise the stakeholder at the end
- **Bring data**, not opinions
- **Keep promises** — particularly high-integrity commitments
- **Acknowledge legitimate constraints** — most stakeholders have real concerns; treat them seriously
- **Iterate** with the stakeholder on framing before committing to direction

## How Stakeholders Build Trust with Product Teams

- **Bring problems**, not solutions
- **Share strategic context** (what's coming in your domain, what's changing in regulations, what's coming from a board commitment)
- **Distinguish suggestions from edicts** clearly
- **Allow time** for discovery
- **Disagree and commit** when the product team chooses a contrarian solution
- **Don't end-run the PM** to a manager or to the CEO

## Stakeholder Briefings

A useful forum: a regular **stakeholder briefing** where product leaders share:
- Product vision and strategy
- Recent discovery findings
- Outcomes against objectives
- Upcoming initiatives

This replaces "approval meetings" with shared understanding. It also lets stakeholders contribute insights at the right altitude.
