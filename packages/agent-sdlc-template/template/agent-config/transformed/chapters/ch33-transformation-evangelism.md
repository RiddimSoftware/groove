# Chapter 33 — Transformation Evangelism

Transformation is never finished. New people join, practices drift, stakeholders look for ways to slide back.

## The Transformation Plan

A working plan — not a Gantt chart — covering:
- Which competencies are being built first
- Which pilot teams and what they're working on
- Which key milestones (e.g. "first product team releasing every two weeks")
- Which stakeholder coaching is underway
- What evangelism rhythm

The plan is **alive and updated**. It's not a project plan.

## Continuous Evangelism

> "Evangelism is something that never finished. It needs to be constant."

Daily in 1:1s, weekly in team meetings, monthly in all-hands, quarterly in board meetings, ongoing in customer briefings — the strategic context and the product model itself must be communicated **constantly**.

The larger the organization, the more relentless this must be.

Topics to evangelize:
- The product vision
- The product strategy and current focus
- Outcomes against objectives
- Discovery learnings
- Successes and lessons from setbacks
- The principles behind the way of working

## The Value of Quick Wins

Quick wins generate the political capital needed for the deeper changes.

Look for visible, measurable, customer-loved improvements that can ship in the first 6–12 weeks of a pilot team's work. Communicate them everywhere:
- All-hands meetings
- Board updates
- Customer briefings
- Sales kickoffs
- Investor calls (when relevant)

A quick win silences a great deal of skepticism.

## Constantly Beat the Drum

The temptation, once the transformation is "underway," is to declare victory and move on.

> "The product culture takes years to build and weeks to lose."

Beating the drum means:
- Repeating the *why* every time a major decision comes up
- Pointing to specific examples of empowered teams delivering outcomes
- Calling out anti-patterns when they appear (a roadmap demand, a feature factory pattern, a stakeholder gating customer access)
- Refusing to use the language of the old model even when it would be easier

## Who Evangelizes

The CEO. The CPO/head of product. Engineering leadership. Design leadership. Every product leader. Product managers. Increasingly, the stakeholders who have seen it work — the strongest evangelists are converts.

The transformation is fragile while only one person is the visible champion. It becomes resilient when many people across functions speak about it naturally.
