# Chapter 20 — Innovation Story: Carmax

A second innovation case study — what becomes possible after a company has moved to the product model.

## Company Background

Carmax is a major U.S. used-car retailer, traditionally a physical-retail business. It transformed to the product model over several years and built strong empowered product teams.

## The Problem to Solve

Buying a used car involved an arduous process: many in-person visits, comparing across dealers, financing complexity, distrust. Carmax wanted to make the experience radically better — and in doing so create a competitive moat that other dealers couldn't replicate.

## Discovering the Solution

The team — equipped with the discovery skills built up during transformation — explored multiple angles:

- **Online inventory** with rich detail and trustworthy information
- **Home delivery** at scale
- **Online financing** built into the experience
- **No-haggle pricing** with clear data backing

Each was tested rapidly with customers. Many concepts that initially seemed obvious turned out to need significant refinement; others initially seen as risky turned out to be loved.

The team's empowered structure — designers, engineers, and PMs collaborating with customers and stakeholders across finance, operations, and sales — was key to making decisions cross-functionally without bottlenecks.

## The Results

Carmax went from a strong but conventional used-car retailer to a digitally-led leader of the category. The brand became known for transparency, ease, and trust — at scale.

The transformation was a multi-year effort, with leadership commitment from the top and ongoing evangelism. The product-model muscles built during transformation continue to drive innovation across new categories of services and customer needs.

## Lessons

- **Industries don't get disrupted just by tech-native startups.** Incumbents who transform can disrupt their own industry from a position of strength — *disrupting themselves before someone else does* (a principle that recurs throughout the book).
- **Cross-functional empowered teams** make rapid, customer-centric experimentation possible where traditional approval chains would have blocked it.
- **Brand value compounds**: a transformation that produces consistent customer-loved improvements creates a moat over years.
