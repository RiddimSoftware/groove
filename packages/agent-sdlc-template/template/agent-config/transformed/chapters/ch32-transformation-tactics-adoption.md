# Chapter 32 — Transformation Tactics — Adoption

How transformation actually enters the organization — beyond the product organization itself.

## Pilot Teams

Pick **one or two pilot teams** to demonstrate the product model before scaling. The pilots:

- Show what is possible — concrete proof in your context
- Generate quick wins that build political capital
- Train the first cohort of product-model practitioners
- Build the case for skeptical stakeholders

Pilots should be chosen carefully:
- A team working on a problem that matters to the business
- With sufficient autonomy to actually exercise the model
- With leadership who genuinely want to learn the new way
- With visible stakeholders who can validate the outcome

## Product Model Dimensions

Recall the three dimensions of transformation:
- Changing how you build
- Changing how you solve problems
- Changing how you decide which problems to solve

Each is a spectrum, not a binary. Most successful transformations work on **all three in parallel** rather than purely sequentially — though the order of investment within each varies by where the company starts.

A typical sequencing:
1. **How you build** comes first. Without small reliable releases, nothing downstream works.
2. **How you solve problems** with at least the pilot team(s) shows what discovery looks like.
3. **How you decide what to solve** matures as product strategy capability develops.

But early progress on strategy (e.g. publishing a coherent product vision) can pull on the other two as well.

## Top Down and Bottom Up

Transformation needs both.

### Top-Down

- CEO and executives visibly support it. *"The company cares about what the leader cares about."*
- Funding aligned to teams and outcomes
- Strategic context shared
- Defensive backing when stakeholders push back on team empowerment

### Bottom-Up

- Product leaders and product teams build new skills
- Demonstrate results visible across the company
- Earn trust through kept promises and shipped outcomes
- Take responsibility for "raising hearts and minds"

Neither alone is sufficient. The CEO can't will the product model into existence by decree. The product team can't build credibility by complaining about the CEO.

## Coaching Stakeholders

Stakeholders are not transformation targets — they are partners learning a new way of working with the product organization.

Areas to coach:
- **Outcome-based roadmaps** — replace feature roadmaps gradually
- **High-integrity commitments** as the exception, not the rule
- **Disagree and commit** when teams propose contrarian solutions
- **Distinguish suggestions from edicts** (especially executives)
- **Bring problems, not solutions**

## Stakeholder Briefings

Regular forums where product teams share strategy, discovery findings, and outcomes with the business. Replaces "approval meetings" with shared understanding. Lets stakeholders contribute insights at the right altitude.

## Managing Existing Commitments

You cannot shut off the prior model overnight. Existing high-integrity commitments must be honored. New commitments should be made in the product-model way.

The transition period requires careful judgment — honor what's promised, but resist new commitments that would entrench the old model.
