# Chapter 28 — Transformation Outcome

The opening chapter of Part VIII (*Transformation Techniques*). Before discussing techniques, the chapter clarifies the desired outcome.

## What Success Looks Like

A company that has successfully moved to the product operating model can:

- **Respond quickly to threats** and take advantage of opportunities
- **Innovate consistently** on behalf of customers
- **Generate substantially better return** on technology investment
- **Retain top engineering, design, and product talent** — the people who want to work this way are exactly the people you want
- **Improve employee morale and retention** across the company, not just inside product

## What Success Does Not Look Like

- A company that has adopted the *titles* of the product model without the competencies
- A company with Agile ceremonies but quarterly releases
- A company that creates an empowered product team but leaves stakeholders untouched
- A company that improves time-to-market but still ships features customers don't use
- A company that hires a CDO and tells them to "go transform"

## Articulating the Outcome

Before tactics, the company needs to be able to answer in one or two sentences: *what will we be able to do after transformation that we can't do today, and why does that matter?*

If the answer is vague, the transformation will lose steam at the first organizational friction point. If the answer is precise, every tactical decision can be evaluated against it.

## Quality vs. Speed of Outcomes

The temptation throughout transformation is to optimize for either:
- **Speed** (rip the band-aid; declare a date for everything to be transformed)
- **Predictability** (a multi-year plan with milestones)

Both are wrong framings. Transformation is **organic**: as competencies grow, concepts can be taken on; as concepts mature, culture stabilizes; as culture stabilizes, the rest of the company can be brought along.

The right framing: **what do we need next to keep momentum**, then act on that — not "what is the four-year Gantt chart."
