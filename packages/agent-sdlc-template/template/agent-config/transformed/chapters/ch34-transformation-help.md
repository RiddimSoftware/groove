# Chapter 34 — Transformation Help

You will likely not have all the in-house expertise needed to coach the new competencies. Four sources of help.

## Managers as Coaches

The first and primary source. Every PM, designer, tech lead, and product leader should have a manager committed to coaching them **weekly**.

In strong product organizations:
- Engineers report to experienced engineering managers
- Designers report to design managers
- PMs report to proven managers of product management

First-level managers spend up to **80% of their workweek** on staffing and coaching. This is not optional — it is the primary job.

## In-House Product Coaches

Some larger companies create dedicated in-house coaching capacity — coaches who work with multiple teams and product leaders. Useful at scale.

**Risk**: a coach from the prior model (e.g., an Agile coach who doesn't understand the product model) can do active harm.

## External Product Coaches

Short-term injection of expertise. Particularly valuable:
- Early in transformation, when in-house coaches don't yet exist
- For product leaders without prior product-model experience
- For specific high-stakes initiatives

## Finding a Product Coach

Vet for:
- **Hands-on experience in the product model** — not consulting frameworks
- **References from successful transformations** — actual companies they helped
- **Coaching style vs. directive style** — coaches help your people grow; consultants do work for them
- **Specialty match** — discovery coaches, leadership coaches, transformation coaches all exist

> "If you are hiring a product leadership coach, they must demonstrate to you that they have leadership experience with product vision, product strategy, team topology, and team objectives at a serious product company."

> "If you are hiring a transformation coach, they must demonstrate to you that they have the experience leading a company through a successful transformation to the product model."

### Beware

> "There exists a whole coaching industry that comes not from product companies, but instead from either specific software processes or management consultancies."

Coaches selling a particular method, framework, or methodology are different from coaches helping you operate in the product model.
