# Chapter 37 — Objections from Sales

Sales partners closely with the product organization. Their objections often stem from the historical dynamic of being the customer's voice and feeling that voice should drive product decisions.

## "We sit with customers every day. Why shouldn't we tell the product team what to build?"

**Response:**
- PMs in the product model **maintain strong relationships** with salespeople and participate in the sales process for key prospects.
- Product teams have direct, ongoing engagement with users — in a **discovery context**, not a sales context.
- Foundational principle: *"The customer and the salesperson do not know what is technically possible."* They are experts in their own domain, not in enabling technology.
- When customers buy your products, they are hiring you to be the experts for them.

## "Great vision, but I need to make my numbers this quarter."

**Response:**
- Provide salespeople with **referenceable customers** using the latest offering and loving it.
- Explain how each product effort connects to direct business impact.

## "I have a promising prospect, but we need to commit to new capabilities to close the deal."

This is **specials**. *One of the greatest fears of product teams.*

**Response:**
- Route all special requests **through the head of sales first** to block most.
- For requests that pass: PM determines if the prospect is in the target market.
- If yes: identify the underlying problem and a solution serving this prospect *and others*.
- If outside target market or strategy: **decline** in favor of the larger opportunity.
- Most special requests stem from lack of referenceable customers — fix that and requests dry up.

## "We lost a deal because we lack feature X."

**Response:**
- Customers say one thing and do another, sometimes unconsciously. Losing prospects often cite an easy excuse.
- Sometimes the feature really is critical. **Reference customers in the segment** are the best way to tell the difference.

## "We keep losing against this key competitor."

**Response:**
- Major product work may be needed.
- Product + product marketing identify **segments where the current offering is superior** and develop reference customers in that segment.
- Communicate timing transparently — major changes may take time.

## "I work hard to manage prospects and customers. I don't want random product teams risking that."

**Response:**
- Direct, unencumbered customer access is **one of the few true non-negotiables** in the product model. CEO may need to make this point directly to sales leadership.
- Acknowledge: there have been cases of inappropriate product-team behavior. Product leaders must coach their people.
- Offer **"charm school"** — short customer interaction training covering roles, expected norms, and the rule that promises made will be kept.

## "I don't want random product teams contacting my customers without coordinating with me."

**Response:**
- Entirely reasonable.
- **Product ops / user research** facilitates and coordinates customer visits — preventing multiple teams from imposing on the same customers and keeping account managers and customer success in the loop.
