# Chapter 24 — Partnering with Finance

Finance — and especially the CFO — is one of the most consequential partners during transformation. The relationship is often initially adversarial: the product model challenges several practices central to how finance has traditionally operated.

## Testing the Product Model

Finance often arrives at the transformation skeptical. Their natural concerns:

- How do we fund teams rather than projects?
- How do we forecast technology spend when ROI per feature is uncertain?
- How do we capitalize software development?
- How do we know which investments are working?

These concerns are legitimate. The product model has answers — but the answers require change on finance's side too.

## Product and Finance Collaboration

A productive partnership has these properties:

- **Team-based funding** replaces project-based funding for ongoing product investment.
- **Outcome-based metrics** replace milestone-based reporting. Teams are accountable for the business outcomes they were assigned.
- **Capitalization** practices adapt — finance teams in product-model companies have figured out how to capitalize software development under the new working model.
- **Quarterly business reviews** with product teams replace stage-gate approvals.

## Requests of Finance

The product organization makes several specific requests of finance:

- Fund **durable product teams**, not projects. This unlocks the discovery/delivery integration that the product model depends on.
- Allow **strategic context** to shape funding — fund the focus areas defined in the strategy, fund teams aligned to them.
- Distinguish **keep-the-lights-on** spend from **new product investment**. Both need funding; both need different oversight.
- Provide finance professionals to **partner with product leaders** on financial planning — not to gatekeep, but to help product teams understand the financial implications of their decisions (unit economics, contribution margin, COGS, etc.).
- Use **OKRs or equivalent** to align product investment with the rest of the company.

## What Finance Gets in Return

In return, the product organization commits to:

- Specific, measurable outcomes tied to each product investment
- Transparent reporting on outcomes — including when they fall short
- Working with finance on unit economics and cost models in discovery, not after build
- Discipline around **high-integrity commitments** when timing is financially material (e.g., launch tied to a quarter)

## Why This Partnership Matters Disproportionately

Finance touches every department. A CFO who genuinely partners with the product organization unblocks much of the rest of the transformation. A CFO who sees product as a cost center to control will block the transformation no matter what the CEO says.

This is one of the most important partnerships to invest in early.
