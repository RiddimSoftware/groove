# Chapter 12 — Tech Leads

## Definition

A "tech lead" is the **most senior individual contributor engineer** on a product team. Not an engineering manager — an IC. Other terms are used (staff engineer, principal engineer, lead engineer). The title doesn't matter; the **mandate** does.

A product team usually has multiple engineers of mixed experience. The book's focus in this chapter is the senior engineer covering the tech lead role on a team.

## Two Dimensions Distinguishing Product-Model Engineers

### Dimension 1: Complexity

Nearly all technology looks easier until you actually get into it. But there are significant differences in product-company complexity:

- Scale and performance
- Fault tolerance and reliability
- Internationalization
- Test automation
- Deployment infrastructure
- Emerging technologies
- Architecture and strategies for managing technical debt

Experience matters because mistakes in these areas are very expensive.

### Dimension 2: Engagement in Problem-Solving

This is the more important dimension and the source of real innovation.

> "Of all the principles underlying the product model, the single most important is the realization that innovation absolutely depends on empowered engineers."

Engineers work with the enabling technology every day, which puts them in the best position to see what's **just now possible**.

But this only translates to innovation if engineers care **just as much about what they build as how they build it**.

The old adage — *"a PM is responsible for the what and why, and the engineer is responsible for the how"* — completely misses the point.

## The Tech Lead's Discovery Mandate

> "If the tech lead is unable or unwilling to engage in product discovery, then you are very likely guaranteeing that the eventual product will not achieve your goals."

This must be a formal part of the tech lead's job description.

In practice, this doesn't require much time — usually **less than an hour a day**. But that hour is critical.

> "A few minutes weighing in on a product idea very early can save weeks or even months of damage control later if decisions are made without consulting with them."

## Other Engineers on the Team

Not all engineers must engage in discovery to the same degree. It's normal for junior engineers to focus primarily on building. You can succeed so long as **at least the tech lead is willing to partner** with the PM and designer to discover an effective solution.

Occasionally the opposite problem arises: engineers want to spend all their time on discovery because they're tired of building "unhelpful features." Remind them their primary job is to build the products in delivery — but it's a good problem to have and easy to correct.

## Why Outsourcing Engineers Fails

The primary reason not to outsource engineers in the product model is that **outsourced engineers are brought in too late and possess too little context** to participate in discovery or have any real chance at innovation.

In an ideal world, all engineers would help determine what the best solution is — not just engaged in building.
