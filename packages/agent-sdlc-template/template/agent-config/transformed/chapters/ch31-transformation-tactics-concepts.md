# Chapter 31 — Transformation Tactics — Concepts

Once new competencies have been established, people are ready to start using their skills on the product concepts.

> "It's important to recognize that skills like product strategy and product discovery can always be improved. There are always new, emerging techniques and tools, and good product companies are always working to improve. So, when you work on transformation tactics, you're not aiming for perfection or even excellence; you're aiming for competence, and then through ongoing coaching, you help your people continue to learn and develop."

## Product Teams

### Team Chemistry

Even if every member has strong skills, the team as a whole may not work well together. Personality clashes or other problems are common. Product leaders **assess the overall chemistry** of each team and either coach or shuffle people to get teams operating effectively.

### Team Durability

Many companies coming from prior models are used to shuffling people between teams on an as-needed basis, especially engineers. In the product model, **durability matters**:

- Moves are only done judiciously and after careful consideration of unintended consequences
- Especially given the level of effort product leaders put into coaching psychological safety and chemistry

### Review Team Topology

> "Team topology refers to how the product teams are structured — especially what each team is responsible for. You can think of it as: How do you slice up the pie?"

Most organizations have **some** team topology already in place, but often it's the **accidental** result of organizational history (Conway's Law) rather than intentional design.

If the company doesn't yet have a team topology — meaning it's still very project-based — transformation begins with the foundational move to **durable, cross-functional product teams**.

If the topology is historical, symptoms appear:
- Long lists of dependencies
- Lack of autonomy and ownership
- Morale issues from people feeling like small cogs in a giant machine

Even if those symptoms aren't present, the topology should be a function of the longer-term product vision and ongoing strategy. If those didn't exist before, almost certainly the topology isn't set up to help achieve them.

Changing topology is very disruptive — don't do it casually or frequently. But the start of a transformation is usually the right time.

## Product Delivery

(Cross-references Chapter 18 — Product Delivery)

The transformation work:
- Move release cadence to **every two weeks at minimum**, ideally continuous deployment
- Invest in **instrumentation, monitoring, and deployment infrastructure**
- A/B test capability, feature flags, rollback systems

For organizations that have to keep the lights on while transforming, target one product or team at a time as a "lighthouse."

## Product Discovery

(Cross-references Chapter 17 — Product Discovery)

- Start using **prototypes, customer testing, A/B tests**
- Discovery should produce **more discarded ideas than items in delivery**; if everything tested gets built, it's design, not discovery
- Cover all four risks: value, viability, usability, feasibility

## Product Strategy

(Cross-references Chapter 16 — Product Strategy)

- Establish a **multi-year product vision** as the unifier
- Develop an **insight-driven product strategy** with 2–3 focus areas
- Translate into **team objectives** (quarterly, problems to solve, measurable outcomes)

## Product Culture

(Cross-references Chapter 19 — Product Culture)

- Replace process gates with **principles + judgment**
- Replace command/control with **strategic context**
- Replace predictability obsession with **innovation discipline** plus disciplined commitments where dates truly matter
- Make **fast/cheap failure** safe and celebrated as learning
