# Chapter 13 — Product Leaders

> "Empowered product teams depend on better leadership, not less leadership."

## The Misconception

Many believe empowerment means managers back off, stop micromanaging, give teams space. Wrong.

Andy Grove's framing:

> "What gets in the way of good work? There are only two possibilities. The first is that people don't know how to do good work. The second is that they know how, but they aren't motivated."

Product leaders tackle both: management (skill) and leadership (motivation, direction).

## Management

### Coaching

> "It is the single most important responsibility of every people manager."

Most overlooked element of strong management. NOT micromanaging. It means:
- Assessing strengths and weaknesses
- Building a coaching plan
- Spending quality time to help each person improve

In strong product orgs: engineers report to experienced engineering managers; designers to design managers; PMs to proven managers of product management. First-level managers spend up to **80% of their workweek** on staffing and coaching.

### Staffing

Managers — not HR — are accountable for:
- Sourcing
- Recruiting
- Interviewing
- Onboarding
- Evaluating
- Promoting
- Replacing team members

HR supports; managers own. *"Setting the bar high in our approach to hiring has been, and will be, the single most important element of Amazon's success."* — Jeff Bezos.

## Leadership: Two Ways to Lead

### Command and Control

Telling people what to do, usually via a roadmap of features and projects. Leaders and stakeholders make meaningful decisions; teams execute. **Easier to do.**

### Empowerment

Assigning business or customer problems to solve and letting teams determine the best way.

> "Lead with context, not control." — Netflix's mantra

Empowerment requires providing **strategic context**: especially the product vision and product strategy.

If a leader finds herself disagreeing with where empowered teams land, or swooping in to change direction, it's worth reflecting on **what context could have been given up front**. This is a skill that requires reflection and improvement.

## What Product Leaders Own

- **Product vision** — the 3–10 year future
- **Team topology** — how teams are structured and what each owns. Maximizes empowerment by being loosely coupled but highly aligned. Avoid Conway's-Law accidents.
- **Product strategy** — focus, insights, bets
- **Team objectives** — typically quarterly. Problems to solve with measurable outcomes.

The litmus test for empowerment: the team can decide the best way to solve their assigned problems (their objectives).

> "It takes strong leaders to be self-confident and secure enough to truly empower the people who work for them and to stand back and let teams take credit for their successes."

## Ongoing Evangelism

> "The larger the organization, the more essential it is to be relentless at evangelism, and it's important for the leaders to understand that evangelism is something that never finished. It needs to be constant."

The final critical role of product leaders is continuous communication of the product model and strategic context — in recruiting, onboarding, 1:1 coaching, all-hands, team lunches, board meetings, customer briefings.

> "As you can see, the job of a product leader in a strong product company based on empowered product teams is both very different from the role in the prior models, and much more difficult."
