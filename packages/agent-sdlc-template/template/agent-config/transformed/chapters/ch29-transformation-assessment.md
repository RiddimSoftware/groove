# Chapter 29 — Transformation Assessment

Before tactics, take honest stock of where the company actually is.

> "It's important to emphasize that the assessment is not assessing individuals; it is assessing the particular model being used to produce products."

Framing matters: a personnel-focused assessment triggers defensiveness, sabotage, or attrition. A model-focused assessment opens the door to *"how can I be part of the change?"*

## Overview

The assessment has two layers:
1. **High-Level Assessment** — broad view of how the organization produces products today, across the three transformation dimensions
2. **Detailed Assessment** — deep look at each product competency and concept

## High-Level Assessment

Three sets of questions, mapped to the three transformation dimensions.

### How Products Are Built and Deployed

- How often do most teams release? Independently, or as a single integrated package?
- If a customer encounters a critical problem, what's the mechanism for detecting and correcting it? What's required for engineers to "release with confidence"?
- Who is responsible for ensuring new capability works correctly? Automated or manual?
- What's the level of autonomy in teams? Dependencies and interactions? Narrow vs. end-to-end responsibility?
- Are new deployments instrumented? Who looks at the data?
- What's the perception of the engineering organization — speed, quality, trustworthiness?
- Technical debt severity, symptoms, plan?

### How Problems Are Solved

- How is work provided to teams? Roadmaps of features and projects? From where?
- When teams begin work, what's the process? Roles? Detailed requirements? Evidence or opinion?
- When and how do engineers and designers enter?
- Stakeholders' role in solution detail?
- Customer interaction before the build decision?
- How often are ideas killed or significantly changed? Who approves?
- Definition of success: shipping? On-time shipping? Outcome achievement? What happens if a feature ships but doesn't achieve the outcome?
- Perception of the people on these teams — knowledgeable about customers/business? Trusted?

### How You Decide Which Problems to Solve

- Who decides what work will get done?
- Annual or quarterly planning process? Establishes priorities and funding? How are funding decisions made? Funds projects or teams?
- Is there a product vision? A product strategy? At what level?
- If teams work off roadmaps, who decides items? Sales? Stakeholders? CEO?
- Items: features or outcomes?
- How is what makes the cut decided? How is the work prioritized?
- Desired business outcomes per item — who defines, how?
- Does the organization track percentage of items that deliver hoped-for results?

## Detailed Assessment

After the high-level view, deep-dive on each competency and concept.

### Product Model Competencies (per role)

- **Size**: get rough numbers per role
- **Product Management**: PM for empowered team vs. feature team vs. delivery team owner? Time use? Customer/data/business/industry knowledge? Training type? Coaching frequency? Stakeholder perception?
- **Product Design**: understanding of service/interaction/visual/industrial design? Enough designers? Embedded vs. internal agency? Wireframe-then-prettify or partner in discovery? Prototype frequency and tools? Experienced design manager providing coaching?
- **Engineering**: senior engineer vs. tech lead distinction? Engaged in discovery? Visiting customers? Outsourced percentages and roles? Engineers responsible for code quality? "Paved paths" for routine tasks?
- **Product Leadership**: people manager or coach + strategic context? Define direction and measures? Evangelize strategic context? Coaching as top responsibility?

### Product Model Concepts

- **Product Teams**: durable or temporary? Delivery / feature / empowered? Cross-functional? Sense of ownership? Access to customers, data, stakeholders?
- **Product Strategy**: multi-year vision? Quarterly planning produces roadmaps, or product leaders produce a strategy of problems to solve based on data?
- **Product Discovery**: design or discovery? Are ideas tested before built? More tested than built? All four risks assessed? Quantitative and qualitative experiments? Responsible testing?
- **Product Delivery**: release frequency — less than every two weeks signals failure. Continuous deployment? Instrumented? Monitored? A/B test infrastructure?
- **Product Culture**: process or principles? Top-down or trust-based? Predictability or innovation? Engineers as innovators? Necessary role of fast/cheap failure?

## Handle With Care

> "If handled well, the assessment can help these people start thinking about how they can be a leader of the changes going forward. If handled poorly, these people may consider leaving the company, or, worse, not leave so they can try to sabotage the transformation."
