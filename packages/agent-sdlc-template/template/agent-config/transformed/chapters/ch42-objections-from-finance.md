# Chapter 42 — Objections from Finance

Finance objections are among the most important to resolve well. Finance touches every department, and a partnered CFO unblocks much of the rest of the transformation.

## "We fund projects, not perpetual teams."

**Response:**
- Funding must evolve from **project-based to product-team-based**.
- This is a real change but not radical — many successful product-model companies have made this shift. The CFO is among the most important transformation partners.
- Project funding optimizes for one-off bursts. Team funding optimizes for ongoing learning, improvement, and ownership of outcomes — what the product model requires.

## "How do I track ROI on continuous investment?"

**Response:**
- Outcome-based metrics: each team is responsible for a set of business outcomes (objectives) tied to the strategy.
- Quarterly business reviews report on outcomes achieved.
- Investment can be increased, held, or decreased based on outcome delivery — not on whether features shipped.

## "How do we capitalize software development?"

**Response:**
- Capitalization rules adapt. Finance teams in product-model companies have figured this out — talk to peers at companies that have transformed.
- The fundamentals don't change; the way work is tracked may. Software development that creates lasting value is generally capitalizable; routine maintenance is generally expensed. The product-model way of working doesn't break this distinction.

## "How do I forecast technology spend?"

**Response:**
- You forecast team capacity (well-known and predictable) and team objectives (what the strategy says).
- Specific feature dates are not the unit of forecast — outcome timing is.
- For investments with hard dates (acquisitions, regulatory deadlines), use **high-integrity commitments**.

## "Some teams won't deliver. How do we cut losses?"

**Response:**
- Quarterly business reviews surface this.
- Failed objectives are a legitimate reason to reorganize teams, change focus, or — if multiple quarters in a row — reduce investment.
- The product model doesn't protect teams from accountability; it focuses accountability on outcomes rather than activity.

## "Our auditors require detailed cost tracking by project."

**Response:**
- Audit and capitalization requirements adapt. Some companies retain project-level tracking for accounting while operating in team-based mode.
- Many product-model companies have navigated this with their auditors. Engage them early.
