# Chapter 8 — Changing How You Solve Problems

The second of three transformation dimensions.

## The Feature Factory Problem

Changing *how* you build is necessary but not sufficient. Many companies just become **more efficient feature factories** — shipping more features but seeing no corresponding value to customers or the business.

> "In most companies today, the percentage of features and projects on product roadmaps that actually generate a positive return on investment is depressingly small. Most industry analysts place it in the range of 10 to 30 percent."

## Why Feature Teams Fail

In the feature-team model:
- Business leaders and stakeholders define a list of features and projects they believe will deliver on business obligations
- They hand these priorities down to feature teams
- Teams produce a product roadmap with dates and deliverables

So why do so few features generate hoped-for returns?

- Each feature is a potential solution to some underlying problem. The stakeholder requesting the feature understands their own need but rarely understands the enabling technology or has the latest customer conversations.
- Designers on feature teams make it look pretty. Engineers build what's requested. **Neither is accountable for the business outcome.**
- If a feature fails, the team points out they built what they were told. The stakeholder argues the feature shipped wasn't what they wanted, or that it took too long. *Lack of trust between stakeholders and feature teams is such a common complaint.*

Another consequence: "orphaned" features — features that don't produce value but are waiting for the day they might get another iteration (which rarely happens). The result is **rapid accumulation of technical debt** that can bring the business to its knees.

## Empower Teams with Problems to Solve

The product model inverts this: rather than a roadmap of features, the team is given a **set of problems to solve and desired outcomes to achieve**.

The team is tasked with finding a solution that is:
- **Valuable** — customer will buy or use it
- **Usable** — user can figure it out
- **Feasible** — engineers can build it
- **Viable** — works for the business (marketing, sales, finance, service, legal, compliance)

## Why Empowered Teams Beat Feature Teams

- **Improved morale** through ownership and direct customer contact
- **Knowledge** from testing solutions with users
- **Empowered engineers** are essential to innovation. They work with the enabling technology daily; they see what's just now possible.

> "When these empowered engineers work collaboratively with product managers and product designers, and are exposed directly to users and customers, you can start to see where nearly every innovative product or service you love originates from."

If you outsource your engineers, they are quite literally mercenaries — and you lose this innovation channel entirely.

## Product Discovery

For the team to perform discovery, they must be cross-functional and have the necessary competencies. See [Chapter 17 — Product Discovery](ch17-product-discovery.md) for techniques.

The discovery rule of thumb: any tested idea should be **at least one order of magnitude cheaper and faster** than building it for real — often two orders of magnitude.

## True Collaboration with Stakeholders

Empowered product teams aren't independent of stakeholders. They are **collaborators**. The relationship shifts from subservient (do what we say) to collaborative (find a solution that works for both customer and business).

Be warned:
- Some stakeholders will resist losing control over technology resources — passively or actively.
- Some of your existing PMs, designers, or engineers may not be willing or able to take on this additional responsibility.

## Outcome-Based Roadmaps

A practical transition tactic: take the existing output-oriented roadmap and, for each item, determine **what problem it's trying to solve and what success measure (outcome) is expected**. You still work on the same problems, but teams now have freedom to explore solutions. This also weans stakeholders off feature/date talk and onto problem/outcome talk.
