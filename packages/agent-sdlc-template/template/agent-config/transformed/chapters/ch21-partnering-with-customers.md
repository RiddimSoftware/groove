# Chapter 21 — Partnering with Customers

The first chapter in Part VI: *The Product Model in Action* — how the product organization constructively partners with the rest of the company and with customers.

## Promises

Customers are the central audience for everything the product team does. In the product model, the relationship has specific promises in both directions.

### What the Product Team Promises Customers

- To do everything possible to solve their problems in ways that work for their business
- To provide ongoing improvements to the product — small, frequent, reliable changes
- To treat data they generate responsibly, instrumenting only what's needed to ensure the product works and helps them solve their problem
- To be transparent about the product vision (where the company is headed)
- To minimize disruption — release changes without forcing constant retraining

### What Customers Are Asked to Provide

- Genuine engagement when invited to participate in discovery
- Honest reactions to prototypes and tests
- Tolerance for the *occasional* miss — discovery means some ideas don't make it

## Product Discovery with Customers

Product managers, designers, and engineers engage directly with users and customers throughout discovery:

- Watching users use the product
- Testing prototypes
- Interviewing about workflows and pain points
- Bringing engineers along (where appropriate) to see real customer struggle firsthand — *"the magic happens"*

Crucially, this is **not** a sales context. It's a discovery context. Customers should know the difference.

## Product Delivery with Customers

The relationship continues during delivery:

- Small, frequent releases keep change manageable
- Feature flags allow gradual rollouts to subsets of users
- Instrumentation reveals real usage patterns
- A/B tests validate that new capability provides expected value

## Happy, Referenceable Customers and Business Impact

The end state — and the test of whether the partnership is working — is **happy, referenceable customers** generating business impact.

When you have referenceable customers, sales has its strongest tool, marketing has its richest stories, and new customers come at lower acquisition cost. This is one of the highest-leverage outcomes of the product model.

If you don't have referenceable customers, that's a leading indicator that you don't have product-market fit (regardless of headcount or revenue), and your transformation has more work to do.
