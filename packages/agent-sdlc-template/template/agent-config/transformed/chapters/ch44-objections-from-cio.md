# Chapter 44 — Objections from the CIO

The CIO has often run technology under the prior (IT) model. Moving to the product model challenges several foundational assumptions about IT's role.

## "Technology serves the business."

**Response:**
- That's the IT model.
- The product model is technology **powering** the business — a fundamentally different relationship.
- In the IT model, stakeholders define what's needed; IT delivers. In the product model, technology is one of the company's primary sources of competitive advantage and innovation, not a service function.
- This shift is uncomfortable for some CIOs and energizing for others. Some CIOs become outstanding CTOs in the new world; some don't make the transition.

## "We have standards and governance."

**Response:**
- Standards stay. The product model doesn't abandon security, compliance, or technical standards.
- **Command-and-control governance** that blocks teams from independent releases must go.
- Engineering managers, "paved paths" (curated default tooling), shared platforms, and architecture review for high-stakes decisions are the product-model way to enforce standards without blocking teams.

## "Engineers can't be customer-facing — they're a cost center."

**Response:**
- In the product model, engineers are an **innovation center**, not a cost center.
- They're the people who see what's just now possible because they work with the enabling technology every day. Cutting them off from customers kills the innovation channel.
- Insourcing is a non-negotiable.

## "We outsource for cost reasons."

**Response:**
- A smaller insourced engineering team consistently outperforms a much larger outsourced team. Insourcing usually results in **cost savings** plus dramatically improved innovation.
- Insourcing takes real time — the tech lead first, then progressively the rest.

## "Engineering reports to me. Why am I losing influence?"

**Response:**
- Engineering still reports to the CIO (or CTO). Engineering managers still own staffing and coaching of engineers.
- What changes is the **relationship between engineering and product/design** — engineers become true partners in solving problems, not implementation labor.
- The CIO's influence increases when engineering becomes a respected partner in business outcomes rather than a back-office function.

## "Our infrastructure can't handle continuous deployment."

**Response:**
- This is a real technical investment, not an objection to deflect.
- Invest in **deployment infrastructure** as part of transformation. The payback is substantial. *Accelerate* is the canonical reference.
- Bring in experienced engineering leaders if internal capability is limited.
