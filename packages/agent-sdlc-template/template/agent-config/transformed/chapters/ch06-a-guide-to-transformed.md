# Chapter 6 — A Guide to TRANSFORMED

## The Promise (and What This Book Is Not)

The book opens by setting expectations:

> "One thing you won't find in this book is any sort of recipe or playbook for transformation. Many people out there will try to sell you such a thing, but unfortunately, we have never seen these one-size-fits-all, overly simplified approaches work."

What the book offers instead:

- **Transformation Stories** — case studies of successful transformations told by the product leaders who led them
- **Innovation Stories** — case studies of what becomes possible after a successful transformation

## Book Structure

| Part | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **II — Transformation Defined** | Comprehensive definition of what transformation actually means |
| **III — Product Model Competencies** | The new skills you need to develop |
| **IV — Product Model Concepts** | The product model principles (foundation for transformation) |
| **VI — The Product Model in Action** | How the product organization constructively partners with the rest of the company |
| **VIII — Transformation Techniques** | The tactics that facilitate transformation |
| **X — Overcoming Objections** | The concerns that arise from each stakeholder |
| **XI — Conclusion** | Common themes and keys to success |

Three full transformation stories (Trainline, Datasite, Adobe) and several innovation stories are interleaved.

## Tough Love for Product Leaders

The chapter ends with direct words for product leaders. Many product leaders complain about:
- Weak product managers
- Not being empowered
- Disengaged engineers
- Distrustful stakeholders
- CEOs who demand detailed roadmaps

> "Yet, they don't seem to realize that each of these problems is very much a consequence of their own actions (or inactions, as the case may be)."

If the product leader is not willing to take responsibility for raising the skill of her product people, product teams will lack competence. When teams lack competence, who would be surprised if:
- Teams are not empowered
- Engineers act like mercenaries
- Stakeholders don't trust PMs
- CEOs don't trust those responsible for weak product people

Two takeaways:
1. You have as much ownership as you have credibility.
2. Part of your job as a product leader is to change hearts and minds.

> "While it is true that the executives have much to change to move to the product model, even larger changes are required of the product leaders and product teams. Our intention is to make very clear the changes that are needed from all sides, but success starts with the product organization raising its game."

## Terminology Note

- **Product managers, product designers, tech leads, engineers, data scientists, user researchers** = individual contributors
- **Product leaders** = managers of product management, product design, and engineering
- **Executives** = CEO, CFO, COO, CMO, CRO, etc.
