# Chapter 17 — Product Discovery

Product discovery is how a team determines whether a particular product idea will work — *before* spending the engineering time and money to build it production-quality.

## Principle 1: Minimize Waste

The single largest source of waste in tech-powered products is **building things customers don't end up using or buying**. Most industry analysts place the proportion of features generating positive ROI at only **10–30%**.

Discovery's primary purpose: evaluate ideas before committing engineering capacity to them.

## Principle 2: Assess Product Risks

Before building, assess the four risks:

| Risk | Question |
|---|---|
| **Value** | Will customers buy or use it? |
| **Viability** | Will it work for the business — marketing, sales, finance, legal, compliance? |
| **Usability** | Can users learn, use, and perceive value? |
| **Feasibility** | Can we build and scale with the staff, time, technology, data we have? |

Different ideas are limited by different risks. The discovery method depends on which risk dominates.

## Principle 3: Embrace Rapid Experimentation

A typical feature takes **3–5 iterations** to reach product-market fit (time to money).

- With a **feature team**, each iteration takes months — meaning 1–2 years before the desired returns, **if** stakeholders keep funding iterations.
- With an **empowered team** practiced in discovery, those 3–5 iterations can happen in days to weeks. The product version is in the customer's hands generating necessary business results just a few weeks after that.

> "If you've ever wondered how small, empowered product teams can so consistently outproduce much larger companies spending much more on their feature teams, this is right at the heart of the answer."

The toolkit:
- **Prototypes** — many forms; all fast and cheap. Designers create a dozen or more per week. Most are thrown away; every one helps converge on a solution.
- **A/B tests** — for live data
- **User testing** — direct observation with real users
- **Wizard of Oz / concierge** — simulating a solution manually before building it

**Rule of thumb**: any discovery prototype should be at least **one order of magnitude cheaper and faster** than building the actual product — often two orders.

## Principle 4: Test Ideas Responsibly

Rapid testing must respect privacy, ethics, and customer trust. Tests that surprise or harm customers undermine the company's brand and may violate law.

## What Discovery Is Not

In organizations that have not transformed, what's called "discovery" is often just **design** — designing what will be built, not assessing whether it should be built.

A telltale: if the number of ideas tested equals the number of items built in delivery, that's design, not discovery. Ideal: testing a larger number of ideas, building less than half of those.
