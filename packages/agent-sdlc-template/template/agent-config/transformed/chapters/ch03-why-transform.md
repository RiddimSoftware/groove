# Chapter 3 — Why Transform?

Given the cost and risk of transformation, companies need a clear answer to *why*. The book identifies three major drivers, often combined.

## Driver 1: A Competitive Threat

Few industries have escaped disruption by new tech-native competitors offering demonstrably better solutions. Recently this has included **Generative AI** reshaping competitive landscapes. The pattern repeats across financial services, healthcare, retail, automotive, logistics, advertising, even space exploration.

Some industries have higher switching costs, regulatory hurdles, or government protection, which slows the disruption — but few companies feel truly secure.

These companies realize they are competing for customers with weapons and strategies that worked yesterday but cannot match the tools and capabilities of their new adversaries.

## Driver 2: A Compelling Prize

Other companies are motivated by the **financial rewards** that flow to companies demonstrating consistent innovation on behalf of customers.

They see:
- Valuations of the new generation of companies
- Rewards to those that have successfully transformed
- The promise of belonging to that cohort

This prize motivates investors, executives, product leaders, and employees.

## Driver 3: Frustrated Leaders

Some companies pursue the product model because they are **fed up with the gap between technology spend and technology return**.

Symptoms:
- Constant cost overruns
- Disappointing results
- Long time-to-ship anything
- Customers losing patience
- Endless blame and excuses

Leaders read about companies that spend less and produce much more, and want to understand if those methods apply to them.

Compounding factors:
- The most capable engineers leaving out of frustration
- A new leader hired from a strong product company who encourages this approach
- Customer expectations changing faster than the company's pace of improvement

## The Underlying Reason

> "Whatever the motivations, fundamentally, companies transform because they believe they need to be able to successfully take advantage of new opportunities that arise and effectively respond to serious threats."

If the company cannot articulate why transformation is necessary in those terms, the effort will not survive the inevitable organizational friction.
