# Chapter 43 — Objections from HR/People Ops

HR and People Operations objections often center on the implications of new roles, expectations, and compensation.

## "Our existing product managers/designers have those titles already."

**Response:**
- **Reset the job definition.** Title does not equal competence in the product model.
- See [Chapter 30 — Transformation Tactics: Competencies](ch30-transformation-tactics-competencies.md) for the job reset technique: temporarily rename people to "product analyst" or "product specialist," then restore the "product manager" title only when capability is demonstrated.

## "We can't pay designers like engineers."

**Response:**
- Compensation must reflect the market.
- Strong product designers in the product model command **engineer-level compensation**. Demand keeps growing as more companies move to the product model.
- This is a real budget shift but it's not optional if you want strong designers.

## "We need career ladders."

**Response:**
- Build career ladders that reflect the **new competencies and behaviors**, not legacy roles.
- A product-model PM career ladder describes: customer/data/business depth, leading discovery, partnering with stakeholders, achieving outcomes — not "writes good requirements documents."

## "We need to evaluate performance."

**Response:**
- Performance evaluation shifts toward **outcomes** rather than output.
- For ICs: did they execute the discovery and delivery competently? Did they grow against their coaching plan?
- For managers: did their team grow? Did their team's outcomes land?

## "Some of our existing people won't be able to make this transition."

**Response:**
- True. Be honest about it.
- Many existing people can be coached to competence; some cannot.
- Identify those who cannot and help them find roles where they will be more successful — at this company or another.
- This is the hard part of transformation and the part that loses companies their nerve. Most failed transformations are afraid to make this call.

## "Hiring is HR's job."

**Response:**
- Hiring is **the hiring manager's job**. HR supports.
- If the hiring manager believes HR can recruit for them, the wrong people will be hired.
- HR provides infrastructure (ATS, scheduling, offers, comp band guidance). The manager owns sourcing, interviewing, evaluating, deciding.
